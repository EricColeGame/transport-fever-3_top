## Core Components of the Timetable Mechanics

To leverage the dispatch engine effectively, you must understand the core parameters controlling vehicle departures. The scheduler does not simply dictate when a train leaves a depot; it controls arrival targets, dwell windows, and platform release timing across every stop on a line.

Configuring the transport fever 3 timetable requires understanding how departure slots interact with track signaling. If a train reaches a station ahead of schedule, it waits quietly at the platform until its allotted departure window opens, keeping downstream blocks clear for express traffic.

### Departure Slots and Off-Peak Modulation

Lines do not experience uniform demand throughout the simulation cycle. Commuters surge during morning and evening rush intervals, whereas mid-day travel stabilizes. 

With advanced departure slots, you can schedule high-frequency departures during peak hours (e.g., every 3 minutes) and drop to wider intervals (e.g., every 6 to 9 minutes) during off-peak periods. This flexibility keeps running costs aligned with real-time passenger volumes.

### Dwell Times and Loading Policies

Dwell time dictates the minimum and maximum duration a vehicle remains docked at a platform. Combining minimum load rules with timetable slots prevents lines from stalling indefinitely when raw materials or passengers run short.

| Timetable Parameter | Function | Recommended Setting | Primary Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Target Departure** | Sets the exact clock interval to leave | Exact minutes on the hour (e.g., :00, :15, :30) | Downstream delays cascade if tight |
| **Minimum Dwell** | Enforces minimum stop duration | 20–30 seconds for local; 45s for express | Platform congestion if set too high |
| **Maximum Dwell** | Caps waiting time for late cargo | Timetable interval minus 15 seconds | Vehicles depart half-empty if cargo stalls |
| **Speed Margin Buffer** | Adds slack to travel times | 10%–15% above fastest theoretical run | Slightly lower theoretical top speed |

## Step-by-Step Guide: Synchronizing Passenger Transfers

The crowning achievement of any public transit manager is the coordinated passenger interchange, often referred to in European transit planning as *Taktfahrplan* (clock-face scheduling). Integrating clock-face scheduling into your transport fever 3 timetable allows multiple feeder lines to drop passengers off simultaneously, exchange riders, and depart without forcing citizens to endure long platform waits.

To design a high-performance passenger interchange hub:

1. **Establish the Hub Anchor:** Designate a central multi-platform terminal where intercity express lines converge with local feeder routes (trams, commuter rail, and bus lines).
2. **Calculate Round-Trip Cycle Times:** Run test vehicles along your branches to calculate the baseline round-trip travel time, including standard acceleration and braking curves.
3. **Pad the Buffer:** Add a 10% buffer to account for adverse weather, signal checks, or minor platform delays.
4. **Align Arrival Symmetries:** Program feeder lines to arrive 2 to 3 minutes prior to the intercity express train.
5. **Set Synchronized Departures:** Schedule departing legs 2 to 3 minutes after the express train arrives, granting travelers sufficient walking time between platforms.

[Express Train Arrives]    -->  00:12
         --- Passenger Exchange Window ---
[Express Train Departs]    -->  00:15
[Feeder Bus Lines Depart]  -->  00:17

## Advanced Signaling, Priority Routes, and Conflict Prevention

A flawless timetable remains vulnerable if your physical track layout lacks proper signaling hierarchy. Optimizing your transport fever 3 timetable alongside block signals eliminates phantom delays at complex junction throats.

Timetables function best when track segments are broken into consistent block lengths. If one block takes 90 seconds to traverse while the adjacent block takes only 20 seconds, the longer block dictates the minimum gap possible between scheduled slots.

| Signaling Strategy | Setup Principle | Impact on Timetabled Lines | Best Suited For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Uniform Block Spacing** | Match signal placement to average braking distance | Stabilizes time required to clear blocks | High-speed dedicated corridors |
| **Overtaking Sidings** | Parallel bypass loops controlled by timed halts | Allows express trains to overtake slower cargo | Shared mixed-traffic mainlines |
| **Flyover Junctions** | Grade-separated track crossings | Prevents crossing moves from blocking opposite-direction paths | Major station throats and mainline splits |
| **Platform Holding Signals** | Signals placed immediately ahead of platform exits | Locks trains until exact timetable departure slot arrives | Multi-line terminal stations |

To maximize throughput, place your platform holding signals close to the platform tip. This ensures that the instant the transport fever 3 timetable clock hits the designated departure second, the train immediately claims the route reservation and begins accelerating without delay.

## Frequently Asked Questions About the Transport Fever 3 Timetable

### How do I prevent train bunching using the transport fever 3 timetable?
To eliminate bunching, open the line management panel, switch the dispatch mode from dynamic headway to fixed scheduling, and assign specific departure slots across major terminals. Ensure you add a 10% travel buffer to each station run so that minor delays do not cascade into subsequent slots.

### Can freight and passenger trains share the same timetable slots?
No. Freight and passenger trains feature vastly different acceleration profiles and top speeds. You should assign passenger trains to fast, frequent intervals (such as every 5 to 10 minutes) and slot slower freight convoys into dedicated off-peak windows or schedule them onto parallel overtaking sidings.

### What should I do if a train falls behind its scheduled departure?
When a vehicle encounters unexpected delays, look at its speed margin buffer. If the schedule has insufficient slack, increase the allowable transit time between stations. In critical cases, you can use the "reset slot" action in the line management window to bump the delayed train to the next available cycle without disrupting following units.

### Does setting strict timetables decrease line profitability?
Initially, fine-tuning timetables requires testing and small fleet adjustments. However, once stabilized, scheduled networks are far more profitable than unmanaged lines. Vehicles spend less time idling with running costs active, passenger transfers increase exponentially, and factories maintain steady production curves.
